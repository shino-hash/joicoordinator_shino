Let's Explpore Japan Together

Hello! I'm Shino Manabe, the Japan Outreach Initiative Program Coordinator at Utah State University. I came to Logan, Utah to share the beauty of Japanese culture with the local community.

My journey with Japanese culture began with my grandmother, who taught calligraphy and flower arranging. I grew up surrounded by seasonal flowers at our home and learned to find calm and focus through calligraphy.

My travels to Indonesia, where I introduced Japanese culture to new audiences for the first time in my life, deepened my appreciation for its richness.

Through workshops, presentations, and interactive activities, I aim to inspire curiosity and make Japanese culture accessible and engaging for everyone.

Weekly Events "Wa-no-Wa" at USU

Wa-no-Wa 2026 Spring

Have you ever been to the Japanese Cultural Hub at Utah State University?

With tatami mats and a warm, homey atmosphere, stepping inside feels like stepping into Japan. There’s almost always someone there—doing homework, eating lunch, taking a quick nap, or playing Japanese games.

But once a week, the room fills with students gathering for one special reason.

That’s Wa-no-Wa,
our weekly mini Japanese culture workshop.

Wa no Wa is a small, weekly program created mainly for USU students minoring in Japanese and those interested in Japan. Through seasonal events, hands-on cultural activities, and casual exposure to the Japanese language, it’s a place to learn, connect, and experience Japan —right on campus.

Come join us and experience Japanese culture, one week at a time.

Here’s what Wa-no-Wa was like in 2025 Fall.

In Fall 2025, Wa-no-Wa explored a wide range of Japanese culture through hands-on workshops—yukata dressing, tea ceremony, furoshiki wrapping, origami, and more.

It wasn’t just about learning. It was about taking a pause from everyday life.

We experienced Japanese culture, learned together, had fun, relaxed, and shared moments of calm. Whether you spoke Japanese fluently, were studying it, or knew almost nothing at all, everyone was welcome—and everyone belonged.

It was a warm, joyful semester where people connected, laughed, and enjoyed Japan together.
